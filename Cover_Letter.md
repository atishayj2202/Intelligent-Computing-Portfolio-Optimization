We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ”Institutional Portfolio Optimization under Cardinality and Execution Frictions: A Walk-Forward Evaluation of Adaptive Diversity Preservation” for consideration as a Research Article in Quantitative Finance (QF).
This manuscript presents an institutional portfolio construction framework based on Adaptive Opposition-Based Learning Symbiotic Organisms Search (AOBL-SOS) for non-convex, cardinality-constrained equity allocation. The main contributions of this work are:
Algorithmic Architecture: The formulation of an institutional Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) portfolio construction engine combining Symbiotic Organisms Search with an exact feasibility-preserving projection M that strictly enforces exact cardinality (K=30) and position caps (W_max=20%) without post-hoc repair heuristics, paired with a stagnation-triggered centroid opposition operator tailored to discontinuous feasible topologies.
Institutional Constraints & Market Impact: The realistic evaluation of operational frictions across seven expanding walk-forward windows (2018–2024) using point-in-time S&P 500 constituent screening, 10 bps annual formation costs, and nonlinear Almgren–Chriss square-root market impact scaling from $10M to $5B AUM, bridging the gap between theoretical allocation models and live institutional execution capacity.
Risk-Aware Objective Function: The optimization of a path-dependent loss function that explicitly penalizes historical maximum drawdown alongside Sharpe ratio, rigorously validated through Ken French/Carhart four-factor attribution (R^2=0.831, identifying the strategy as an institutional factor harvester with conditional residual alpha=6.53%), Combinatorial Symmetric Cross-Validation establishing moderate parameter stability (PBO=0.427, DSR=0.956), and stationary block-bootstrap intervals.
Extensive experimental studies demonstrate that the proposed AOBL-SOS engine achieves superior out-of-sample risk-adjusted performance (chained net Sharpe 0.919, net CAGR 21.55%, Calmar ratio 0.616) over baseline metaheuristics (SOS Sharpe 0.792, PSO 0.859) and analytical benchmarks (Ledoit–Wolf 0.713, 1/N 0.700), while reducing maximum drawdown from –36.05% to –31.75%. Econometric and statistical analyses (pairwise Jobson–Korkie/Memmel tests, stationary block bootstrap, CSCV PBO, and Carhart factor regressions) further confirm the economic value, scalability, and robust parameter stability of the obtained results across 8 competing baselines.
We believe that our work is well aligned with the aims and scope of Quantitative Finance, as it advances the empirical and quantitative methodology for institutional portfolio construction under non-convex operational constraints, market impact frictions, and model-selection risk. The manuscript will be of interest to researchers and practitioners working in quantitative asset management, financial econometrics, market microstructure, algorithmic execution, and computational portfolio optimization.
