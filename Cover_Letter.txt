Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ”Institutional Portfolio Optimization under Cardinality and Execution Frictions: A Walk-Forward Evaluation of Adaptive Diversity Preservation” for consideration as a Research Article in Quantitative Finance (QF).

This manuscript presents Adaptive Opposition-Based Learning Symbiotic Organisms Search (AOBL-SOS) for high-dimensional, non-convex portfolio optimization. The main contributions of this work are:

● Algorithmic Architecture: The formulation of an institutional MINLP engine combining Symbiotic Organisms Search with an exact feasibility-preserving projection M (strictly enforcing K=30 cardinality and W_max=20% caps without heuristic repair) and a stagnation-triggered centroid opposition operator tailored to non-convex topologies.

● Institutional Constraints: The realistic evaluation of operational frictions across seven expanding walk-forward windows (2018–2024), incorporating 10 bps annual formation costs and nonlinear Almgren–Chriss market impact up to $5B AUM, bridging theoretical search and institutional execution.

● Risk-Aware Objective Function: The optimization of a path-dependent fitness function actively penalizing maximum drawdown alongside Sharpe ratio, proving the framework natively navigates severe market regime shifts while preserving portfolio solvency and mandate feasibility.

Extensive walk-forward evaluations demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior out-of-sample risk-adjusted performance (chained net Sharpe 0.919, net CAGR 21.55%, Calmar ratio 0.616) and reduced maximum drawdown (–31.75% vs –36.05%) across 8 competing baselines. Econometric and statistical analyses (Carhart 4-factor attribution, CSCV PBO, and Jobson–Korkie bootstrap tests) further confirm significant economic outperformance, institutional scalability, and parameter stability.

We believe that our work aligns closely with the aims of Quantitative Finance, advancing quantitative methodology for portfolio allocation under non-convex operational constraints, execution frictions, and model-selection risk. The manuscript will interest researchers and practitioners working in quantitative asset management, financial econometrics, and computational finance.
