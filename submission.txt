Quantitative Finance submission items
====================================

Title
Institutional Portfolio Construction under Cardinality Friction:
An Adaptive Diversity-Preserving Walk-Forward Study

Corresponding author
Atishaya Jain, Department of Computer Science and Engineering,
Netaji Subhas University of Technology (NSUT), New Delhi, India
atishaya.jain.ug23@nsut.ac.in

Keywords
cardinality-constrained portfolio optimization; walk-forward evaluation;
market impact; model-selection risk; adaptive diversity preservation;
Carhart attribution; implementation shortfall

JEL Codes
C61; G11; C63; G17

Highlights
1. Adaptive diversity preservation is formulated as an institutional MINLP
   portfolio construction engine enforcing exact cardinality (K=30) and
   position caps (W_max=20%) without post-hoc repair heuristics.
2. Expanding point-in-time walk-forward tests (2018-2024) across seven OOS
   windows eliminate survivorship bias; locked annual weights hold with intra-year drift.
3. AOBL-SOS delivers superior out-of-sample risk-adjusted performance
   (chained Sharpe 0.919, net CAGR 19.55%, Calmar 0.616) over baseline metaheuristics
   (SOS Sharpe 0.792, PSO 0.859) and analytical benchmarks (Ledoit-Wolf 0.713, 1/N 0.700).
4. Ken French/Carhart attribution confirms statistically significant selection alpha
   (alpha = 6.53%, t=2.01, p=0.045) with R^2 = 0.831; the 2022 drawdown reflects
   macro interest rate tightening market beta and growth factor transmission.
5. Multiple-testing validation yields low Probability of Backtest Overfitting
   (PBO = 0.427) and DSR = 0.956; Almgren-Chriss capacity scaling shows minimal
   shortfall (0.15 pp CAGR drag at $5B AUM).

Distinct from the SWEVO manuscript
The SWEVO paper studies AOBL-SOS as a generic evolutionary search algorithm tested
on benchmark mathematical functions. This Quantitative Finance manuscript investigates
institutional portfolio construction under MINLP cardinality bounds, point-in-time
walk-forward dynamics, Almgren-Chriss market impact, CSCV/DSR overfitting control,
and Carhart 4-factor risk attribution.
