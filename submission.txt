Title

Institutional Portfolio Optimization under Cardinality and Execution Frictions:
A Walk-Forward Evaluation of Adaptive Diversity Preservation

Abstract (119 words)
-------------------
Institutional portfolio selection with exact cardinality (||w||0 = K), position concentration caps, and path-dependent drawdown penalties is an NP-hard mixed discrete–continuous problem that cannot be addressed by classical quadratic programming and standard metaheuristics are known to suffer from the premature diversity collapse. We propose the Adaptive Opposition-Based Learning Symbiotic Organisms Search (AOBL-SOS) institutional construction framework, which has two structural mechanisms: the projection back into the capped cardinality simplex is exact and preserves the feasibility of the individuals, without relying on post-hoc repair heuristics; and the individual is calculated as the centroid of the population for a discontinuous feasibility topology, when stagnation occurs. The adaptive engine earns a chained out-of-sample Sharpe ratio of 0.919, an annual compounded gross of 21.55% and a Calmar ratio of 0.616 at 10 bps of annual formation friction per point-in-time S&P 500 cross-section evaluated over a 7-window expanding walk-forward horizon (2018–2024). This substantially improves upon baseline SOS (0.792, 19.06%) and PSO (0.859, 20.64%) while reducing maximum drawdown (–31.75% vs –36.05%). Nonlinear Almgren-Chriss execution modelling matches the sound institutional scalability to $5B AUM with minimum shortfall (0.15 pp CAGR drag). Econometric attribution shows that the strategy functions as a systematic factor harvester (R^2 = 0.831) with a marginally significant conditional residual alpha (alpha = 6.53%, p=0.045), while Combinatorial Symmetric Cross-Validation establishes moderate parameter stability (PBO = 0.427, DSR = 0.956).

Significance Statement / Relevance to Intelligent Computing
-----------------------------------------------------------
This manuscript addresses a central challenge in computational intelligence and heuristic optimization: navigating high-dimensional, discontinuous search spaces subject to strict discrete-continuous constraints without suffering premature diversity collapse. By introducing an exact continuous-to-discrete feasibility projection mapping M onto capped cardinality simplices and coupling it with stagnation-conditioned centroid opposition, our framework resolves the acute diversity decay that plagues standard bio-inspired swarms (GA, PSO, DE, SOS). We validate this algorithmic breakthrough on a demanding, real-world benchmark: expanding 7-year out-of-sample institutional portfolio selection across the point-in-time S&P 500 universe with nonlinear market microstructure frictions. This work demonstrates how adaptive diversity mechanisms bridge the gap between theoretical evolutionary computation and high-stakes operational decision systems.

Research Highlights
-------------------
1. Formulates an adaptive bio-inspired search engine (AOBL-SOS) featuring an exact feasibility projection M mapping continuous organisms onto capped cardinality simplices without heuristic repair.
2. Introduces a stagnation-triggered centroid opposition mechanism that dynamically reflects organisms to postpone diversity collapse across non-convex discontinuous manifolds.
3. Evaluates 7-year expanding walk-forward out-of-sample performance across point-in-time S&P 500 cross-sections, achieving superior risk-adjusted return (Sharpe 0.919, CAGR 21.55%, Calmar 0.616) over baseline metaheuristics (SOS 0.792, PSO 0.859).
4. Demonstrates robust institutional scalability under nonlinear Almgren-Chriss market impact up to $5B AUM with only 0.15 pp CAGR shortfall.
5. Establishes parameter stability and controlled model-selection risk via Combinatorial Symmetric Cross-Validation (PBO = 0.427, DSR = 0.956) and Carhart four-factor attribution (R^2 = 0.831, alpha = 6.53%, p=0.045).
